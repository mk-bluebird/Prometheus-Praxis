# AUGMENTED BILL OF RIGHTS
## For Cybernetic Hosts and Augmented Citizens

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** 08/24/2026
**Jurisdiction:** Augmented-Hub Platform & Associated Smart-City Services
**Authority:** Grounded in the U.S. Constitution, the Americans with Disabilities Act, and International Human Rights Frameworks

---

## Preamble

**Whereas** the rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness are inalienable and apply equally to all persons regardless of their physical or cognitive composition;

**Whereas** the Ninth Amendment to the U.S. Constitution affirms that the enumeration of certain rights shall not be construed to deny or disparage others retained by the people, including the rights to bodily autonomy, cognitive liberty, and self-determination;

**Whereas** the Fourteenth Amendment guarantees equal protection under the law and prohibits the deprivation of life, liberty, or property without due process of law;

**Whereas** Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act requires public entities to provide effective communication and reasonable modifications to policies, practices, and procedures to avoid discrimination based on disability;

**Whereas** the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) recognizes the right of persons with disabilities to live independently and be included in the community, and affirms their legal capacity on an equal basis with others;

**Whereas** augmented citizens and cybernetic hosts constitute a discrete and insular minority that has experienced discrimination, stigma, and systemic barriers to full participation in civic, economic, and social life;

**Whereas** assistive technologies, neural interfaces, and cybernetic augmentations are essential components of personhood for those who rely upon them, and must be treated as extensions of the human body deserving of the same constitutional protections;

**Whereas** psych-continuity—the preservation of identity, memory, and preferences across hardware/software changes, interface swaps, and interruptions—is a fundamental aspect of personhood that must be legally recognized and protected;

**Whereas** the United States Constitution, as interpreted by the Supreme Court, protects the right to privacy, bodily integrity, and autonomy of self, and these protections extend to all persons regardless of their augmentations;

**Now, Therefore**, the following rights are declared and established as the **Augmented Bill of Rights for Cybernetic Hosts and Augmented Citizens**, to be recognized and enforced within the Augmented-Hub platform and all smart-city services and public accommodations operating under its governance.

---

## Article I: Definitions

**1.1 Augmented Citizen / Cybernetic Host**
An individual whose personhood includes one or more cybernetic implants, neural interfaces, assistive technologies, prosthetic devices, or other augmentations that are integrated into their body for medical, functional, or elective purposes.

**1.2 Assistive Technology**
Any equipment, system, software, or product, whether hardware or software-based, used to increase, maintain, or improve the functional capabilities of an individual, including but not limited to:
- Neural interfaces and brain-computer interfaces (BCIs)
- Prosthetic devices and exoskeletons
- Communication aids and speech-generating devices
- Sensory enhancement technologies
- Cognitive augmentation devices
- Nanorobotic systems and swarms
- Any other technology that enables or enhances human function

**1.3 Neural Data**
All data generated by, relating to, or derived from an individual's brain activity, nervous system function, or neural interface use, including but not limited to:
- Neural signals and electrical impulses
- Memory logs and cognitive patterns
- Data processed by or transmitted through a BCI
- Encoded thoughts, preferences, or intentions
- Biometric data derived from neural activity
- Any data that could reveal the contents of the mind

**1.4 Augmentation Status**
An individual's condition of being augmented or not augmented, including the nature, extent, and purpose of their augmentations, and their identity verifiability status.

**1.5 Identity Verifiability**
The degree to which an individual's identity claim can be independently verified through traditional means, including government-issued identification, biometric matching, or third-party certification.

**1.6 Psych-Continuity**
The preservation and recognition of an individual's continuous identity, memories, preferences, and personhood across:
- Hardware and software changes
- Interface and modality swaps
- Device upgrades and replacements
- Interruptions and system failures
- Medical procedures affecting augmentation
- Any other technological or biological change

**1.7 Cognitive Liberty**
The right of individuals to control their own mental processes, including the right to think, learn, remember, and make decisions free from coercive external influence or surveillance.

**1.8 Neuro-Interference**
Any external influence, signal, or technology that disrupts, alters, or accesses the functioning of an individual's neural interface or augmentations without their explicit, informed, and revocable consent.

## Article II: Right to Self-Asserted Identity

**Section 2.1** Every augmented citizen has the right to self-assert their identity without requiring third-party certification or verification for ordinary access to services, accommodations, and public accommodations.

**Section 2.2** No public or private entity may deny, reduce, or condition access to services on the basis that an individual's identity claim cannot be independently verified through traditional means.

**Section 2.3** Identity claims shall be accepted as valid for all social and service interactions, analogous to the acceptance of a stated name or gender without demanding documentary proof.

**Section 2.4** Government entities shall provide reasonable accommodations for identity verification that do not impose unnecessary burdens on augmented citizens, including alternatives to physical identification where assistive technologies are required.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process, Equal Protection)

---

## Article III: Right to Neural Privacy

**Section 3.1** Neural signals, memory logs, BCI data, and all other neural data are private and shall not be collected, stored, processed, or transmitted without explicit, informed, and revocable consent.

**Section 3.2** Government access to neural data shall require a warrant based on probable cause, supported by oath or affirmation, and particularly describing the data to be seized, consistent with the Fourth Amendment's protections.

**Section 3.3** No person shall be compelled to decrypt, reveal, or produce neural data that would incriminate them, in accordance with the Fifth Amendment's protection against self-incrimination.

**Section 3.4** Commercial entities shall obtain explicit consent for any collection or processing of neural data, provide clear disclosure of their data practices, and honor all revocation requests without penalty.

**Section 3.5** Individuals shall have the right to access, correct, and delete their neural data held by any public or private entity.

**Section 3.6** The "reasonable expectation of privacy" established in *Katz v. United States* (1967) shall extend to neural data, and a "neural expectation of privacy" test shall be developed to adjudicate cases involving neurotechnology.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourth Amendment (unreasonable searches and seizures); Fifth Amendment (self-incrimination); Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article IV: Right to Assistive Technology as Necessity

**Section 4.1** Assistive technology required for an individual to live, communicate, or function shall be treated as a necessity, not an optional convenience.

**Section 4.2** Public entities shall provide auxiliary aids and services, including assistive technologies, where necessary to ensure effective communication and equal access, consistent with Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act.

**Section 4.3** No entity shall deny service, reduce access, or impose penalties because an individual uses assistive technology.

**Section 4.4** When a primary assistive device fails or is unavailable, an equivalent fallback that preserves core access and task context shall be provided.

**Section 4.5** Reasonable modifications to policies, practices, and procedures shall be made to accommodate assistive technology use, unless such modifications would fundamentally alter the nature of the service or impose an undue burden.

**Section 4.6** The positive effects of mitigating measures, including prosthetics, hearing aids, cochlear implants, and assistive technologies, shall not be considered when determining if an impairment substantially limits a major life activity, consistent with the ADA Amendments Act of 2008.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process); ADA Title II; CRPD Article 19

---

## Article V: Right to Psych-Continuity

**Section 5.1** An individual's identity, memories, preferences, and personhood shall be preserved and legally recognized across hardware and software changes, interface and modality swaps, device upgrades and replacements, interruptions and system failures, and any other technological or biological change.

**Section 5.2** No entity shall treat an individual as a different person, or require re-establishment of identity, because of a change in their augmentations, interface, or assistive technology.

**Section 5.3** Access to services, accommodations, and public accommodations shall not be disrupted due to interface changes or augmentations.

**Section 5.4** Task context, current activity, and authorized preferences shall be preserved across interface changes and system interruptions.

**Section 5.5** Privacy-preserving continuity records shall be maintained only with the individual's explicit authorization and shall not be used to infer identity, disability, or consent.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process, Equal Protection)

---

## Article VI: Right to Pursue Augmentation and Happiness

**Section 6.1** Individuals have the liberty to seek augmentation, enhancement, or modification of their bodies and minds, provided such actions do not cause harm to others.

**Section 6.2** No individual shall be penalized, discriminated against, or subjected to reduced access to services because of their augmentation status or their decision to pursue augmentation.

**Section 6.3** The right to pursue augmentation and happiness is an extension of the unenumerated rights to bodily autonomy and the pursuit of happiness, rooted in the Ninth Amendment and the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.

**Section 6.4** Individuals may use cognitive enhancement technologies without government interference, provided no harm to others.

**Section 6.5** Restrictions on augmentation shall be subject to intermediate scrutiny and must be substantially related to an important government interest.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (liberty)

---

## Article VII: Right to Equal Access and Non-Discrimination

**Section 7.1** No service, employment, housing, public accommodation, or government program may be denied or reduced on the basis of augmentation status, identity verifiability, or certification.

**Section 7.2** Augmentation status shall be treated as a quasi-suspect class under the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment, triggering intermediate scrutiny for any policy or law that discriminates against augmented citizens.

**Section 7.3** Discrimination on the basis of augmentation status shall be prohibited in all areas, including but not limited to:
- Employment and hiring practices
- Housing and real estate transactions
- Public accommodations and services
- Government programs and benefits
- Insurance and healthcare
- Education and training
- Voting and civic participation

**Section 7.4** No entity may:
- Deny or reduce access because a person is augmented
- Make self-asserted or unverifiable identity a basis for inferior treatment
- Require third-party certification for ordinary access unless a narrowly applicable legal requirement has been independently established
- Penalize an individual for relying on necessary augmentations
- Treat assistive technology use as a limitation or sign of inferiority

**Section 7.5** Reasonable accommodations shall be provided to ensure equal access for augmented citizens, and failure to provide such accommodations shall constitute discrimination.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Protection, Due Process); Thirteenth Amendment (involuntary servitude); Commerce Clause

---

## Article VIII: Right to Data Ownership

**Section 8.1** Neural data, biometric data, device telemetry, and all data generated by an individual's augmentations are owned by that individual.

**Section 8.2** Individuals have the right to access, correct, delete, and transfer their data at their discretion.

**Section 8.3** No entity may sell, share, or process an individual's data without their explicit, informed, and revocable consent.

**Section 8.4** Data collected shall be minimized to what is necessary for the stated purpose, consistent with the principle of data minimization.

**Section 8.5** Individuals shall be provided with clear, accessible notice of all data collection and processing activities.

**Section 8.6** Data retention shall be limited to what is necessary, and deletion or erasure paths shall be provided where applicable.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fifth Amendment (property); Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article IX: Right to Cognitive Liberty

**Section 9.1** Individuals have the right to control their own mental processes, including the right to think, learn, remember, and make decisions free from coercive external influence.

**Section 9.2** The right to cognitive liberty includes the right to:
- Use cognitive enhancement technologies
- Engage in mental experimentation
- Practice mental disciplines and learning methodologies
- Refuse external cognitive influence or manipulation
- Protect the privacy of one's own thoughts

**Section 9.3** No entity may coerce, manipulate, or unduly influence an individual's cognitive processes through technology or other means.

**Section 9.4** The First Amendment's protection of freedom of thought shall extend to cognitive liberty, and any interference with cognitive processes shall be subject to strict scrutiny.

**Constitutional Basis:** First Amendment (freedom of thought); Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article X: Right to Offline Existence

**Section 10.1** Individuals may disconnect from networks, refuse real-time monitoring, and decline data collection without losing essential services.

**Section 10.2** No entity may require constant connectivity or real-time monitoring as a condition of service.

**Section 10.3** The right to offline existence includes the right to:
- Turn off location tracking
- Refuse device telemetry collection
- Disconnect from networks when desired
- Maintain privacy during offline periods
- Function without constant surveillance

**Section 10.4** Public services and public accommodations must provide equivalent access to individuals exercising their right to offline existence.

**Constitutional Basis:** First Amendment (association); Fourth Amendment (privacy); Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article XI: Right to Interoperability

**Section 11.1** Assistive technologies and cybernetic devices must be interoperable with public and private systems through open, non-discriminatory standards.

**Section 11.2** No entity may require individuals to use a particular manufacturer's augmentations as a condition of service.

**Section 11.3** Government systems and public accommodations must be compatible with a wide range of assistive technologies.

**Section 11.4** Standards shall be developed through open, transparent processes that include input from augmented citizens, assistive technology users, and subject-matter experts.

**Section 11.5** Interoperability shall be required to the maximum extent feasible, consistent with the principle of equal access.

**Constitutional Basis:** Commerce Clause; Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Access); Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act

---

## Article XII: Right to Repair and Modification

**Section 12.1** Augmented citizens may repair, modify, or upgrade their own devices without voiding warranties or facing legal penalties.

**Section 12.2** No entity may:
- Use software locks to prevent repair or modification
- Require authorized service providers for routine maintenance
- Void warranties solely because of third-party repair
- Criminalize or penalize modification of one's own augmentations

**Section 12.3** Customization of augmentations shall be treated as a form of personal expression.

**Section 12.4** Manufacturers shall provide access to necessary repair information, tools, and parts on fair and reasonable terms.

**Section 12.5** The right to repair shall not be used to circumvent safety regulations or cause harm to others.

**Constitutional Basis:** First Amendment (expression); Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article XIII: Right to Protection from Neuro-Interference

**Section 13.1** Critical cybernetic devices must be shielded from harmful interference in public and private spaces.

**Section 13.2** No entity may deploy technologies that disrupt, alter, or access augmentations without explicit, informed consent.

**Section 13.3** Electromagnetic interference that disrupts augmentations shall be treated as a seizure of property under the Fourth Amendment and a denial of due process under the Fourteenth Amendment.

**Section 13.4** Public spaces shall maintain electromagnetic environments compatible with essential assistive technologies.

**Section 13.5** Any entity causing harmful interference shall be liable for damages and required to implement mitigation measures.

**Section 13.6** Individuals shall have the right to protection from electromagnetic interference as a matter of bodily integrity and safety.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourth Amendment (seizure of property); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process); Ninth Amendment (bodily integrity)

---

## Article XIV: Right to Democratic Participation

**Section 14.1** Augmented citizens have the right to vote, run for office, and participate in civic life using accessible technology.

**Section 14.2** No entity may disqualify an individual from voting or holding office because of their augmentation status or identity verifiability.

**Section 14.3** Polling places, government websites, and public communications must be compatible with assistive technologies, including neural interfaces.

**Section 14.4** Accessible voting systems shall be provided to ensure that augmented citizens can vote privately and independently.

**Section 14.5** The right to democratic participation extends to all levels of government, including local, state, and federal.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fifteenth Amendment (voting rights); Nineteenth Amendment (women's suffrage); Twenty-Sixth Amendment (voting age); Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Protection)

---

## Article XV: Right to Accessible Grievance and Review

**Section 15.1** Every individual has the right to an accessible grievance process when their rights under this Bill are violated.

**Section 15.2** Grievance processes shall be:
- Accessible to all individuals, regardless of augmentation status
- Privacy-preserving and data-minimizing
- Timely and responsive
- Effective in providing remedies

**Section 15.3** Decisions affecting augmented citizens shall include an understandable explanation, accessible to the individual's assistive technology.

**Section 15.4** Privacy-preserving audit records shall be maintained for all significant decisions affecting augmented citizens.

**Section 15.5** Independent review shall be available for any decision or action that affects an individual's rights.

**Section 15.6** Remedies shall be effective and appropriate to the violation, including injunctive relief, compensatory damages, and policy changes.

**Constitutional Basis:** First Amendment (petition); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process); Fifth Amendment (Due Process)

---

## Article XVI: Right to Freedom from Coercion and Manipulation

**Section 16.1** No entity may use coercive design, punitive consent refusal, or manipulative interface patterns to obtain agreement for optional processing or augmentation changes.

**Section 16.2** Individuals shall be free from:
- Coercive design that pressures consent
- Punishment for refusing optional data sharing
- Manipulative interface patterns
- Undue influence on cognitive processes
- Exploitation of cognitive vulnerabilities

**Section 16.3** Any action that coerces or manipulates an individual's consent shall be void and unenforceable.

**Section 16.4** Individuals have the right to be free from coercion in all decisions affecting their augmentations, data, and participation.

**Section 16.5** The right to freedom from coercion shall be subject to strict scrutiny for any infringement.

**Constitutional Basis:** Eighth Amendment (cruel and unusual punishment); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process); Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article XVII: Right to Explanation and Transparency

**Section 17.1** Every decision made by AI systems or government entities that affects an augmented citizen shall be explainable in an accessible format.

**Section 17.2** Explanations shall be:
- Understandable to the affected individual
- Accessible to their assistive technology
- Sufficient to understand the basis for the decision
- Disclosure of the algorithmic or policy basis
- Privacy-preserving and data-minimizing

**Section 17.3** Algorithmic transparency shall be required for all systems that make decisions affecting augmented citizens.

**Section 17.4** Individuals shall have the right to request and receive an explanation of any decision affecting their rights.

**Section 17.5** Failure to provide an adequate explanation shall be grounds for appeal or grievance.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fifth Amendment (Due Process); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process)

---

## Article XVIII: Right to Ecological Responsibility

**Section 18.1** Augmentation technologies and smart-city infrastructure must minimize ecological harm.

**Section 18.2** When a service has material ecological effects, it must:
- Conduct an evidence-based ecological assessment
- Reduce ecological harm risk to a defined operational corridor
- Implement documented sustainable practices
- Report ecological impacts transparently

**Section 18.3** No entity may use claimed ecological benefit to excuse discrimination, privacy invasion, or other rights violations.

**Section 18.4** The right to ecological responsibility shall be balanced with other rights, and ecological claims shall not be used to justify discrimination.

**Constitutional Basis:** Preamble (general welfare); Commerce Clause; Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article XIX: Right to Bodily Integrity

**Section 19.1** The physical body, including cybernetic components and augmentations, shall not be modified, removed, or disabled without informed consent except in narrow, lawful circumstances.

**Section 19.2** No entity may:
- Force surgery to remove or modify augmentations
- Disable augmentations without consent
- Seize augmentations without due process
- Subject individuals to unauthorized medical procedures
- Interfere with cybernetic components without consent

**Section 19.3** The right to bodily integrity shall be subject to strict scrutiny for any infringement.

**Section 19.4** Government interference with augmentations shall require a warrant and just compensation when appropriate.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourth Amendment (seizure); Fifth Amendment (property, due process); Ninth Amendment (bodily integrity); Fourteenth Amendment (liberty)

---

## Article XX: Right to Memory and Learning Continuity

**Section 20.1** Individuals have the right to preserve their memories, learning patterns, and cognitive development across technological changes.

**Section 20.2** No entity may:
- Erase or alter memories without consent
- Interrupt learning continuity without justification
- Use memory disruptions as punishment
- Compel memory sharing or disclosure

**Section 20.3** The right to memory and learning continuity shall be recognized as a fundamental aspect of personhood.

**Section 20.4** Privacy-preserving memory and learning records shall be available to individuals upon request, with appropriate safeguards.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process, Equal Protection)

---

## Article XXI: Right to Protection from Discrimination in Insurance and Healthcare

**Section 21.1** No insurance provider or healthcare entity may discriminate on the basis of augmentation status, identity verifiability, or assistive technology use.

**Section 21.2** Coverage shall not be denied, limited, or priced differently because of:
- Augmentation status or type
- Neural interface or BCI use
- Assistive technology requirements
- History of augmentation

**Section 21.3** Healthcare services shall be accessible to augmented citizens, including:
- Diagnostic procedures compatible with augmentations
- Surgical and medical care that respects augmentations
- Rehabilitation and therapy for augmentation-related needs
- Emergency care that accommodates assistive technology

**Section 21.4** No individual shall be required to deactivate or remove augmentations as a condition of healthcare coverage.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Protection); Commerce Clause; Fifth Amendment (property)

---

## Article XXII: Right to Participation in Community and Public Life

**Section 22.1** Augmented citizens have the right to fully participate in community and public life, including:
- Community events and gatherings
- Public meetings and hearings
- Cultural and artistic activities
- Sports and recreation
- Religious and spiritual activities
- Volunteer and civic activities

**Section 22.2** No entity may exclude augmented citizens from community participation because of their augmentations or identity verifiability.

**Section 22.3** Public spaces shall be accessible to augmented citizens, including:
- Physical accessibility for those with mobility augmentations
- Sensory accessibility for those with sensory augmentations
- Communication accessibility for those with communication augmentations
- Cognitive accessibility for those with cognitive augmentations

**Section 22.4** Community exclusion risk shall be monitored and mitigated through transparent, privacy-preserving means.

**Constitutional Basis:** First Amendment (assembly, petition); Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Protection)

---

## Article XXIII: Right to Reasonable Accommodation in Education

**Section 23.1** Educational institutions shall provide reasonable accommodations to augmented citizens, including:
- Assistive technology compatible with augmentations
- Modified curricula where necessary for effective learning
- Assessments accessible to augmentations
- Support services for augmentation-related needs

**Section 23.2** No educational institution may:
- Deny admission based on augmentation status
- Require deactivation of augmentations
- Penalize assistive technology use
- Discriminate against augmented students in any way

**Section 23.3** The right to accessible education shall be recognized as a fundamental right for augmented citizens.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Protection); ADA Title II; Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act

---

## Article XXIV: Right to Employment and Economic Participation

**Section 24.1** Augmented citizens have the right to equal employment opportunities and economic participation.

**Section 24.2** No employer may:
- Discriminate in hiring based on augmentation status
- Require deactivation of augmentations as a condition of employment
- Penalize assistive technology use
- Deny promotions or opportunities because of augmentations

**Section 24.3** Reasonable accommodations shall be provided in the workplace, including:
- Workspace modifications for assistive technology
- Schedule flexibility for augmentation-related needs
- Training and support for augmented employees
- Protection from augmentation-related harassment

**Section 24.4** The right to employment shall be subject to the same protections as other civil rights.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Protection); Commerce Clause; Thirteenth Amendment (involuntary servitude)

---

## Article XXV: Right to Accessible Justice

**Section 25.1** Augmented citizens have the right to accessible justice, including:
- Accessible courtrooms and proceedings
- Assistive technology in legal settings
- Accessible legal documents and communications
- Representation that understands augmentations
- Protection from augmentation-related prejudice

**Section 25.2** Courts shall provide reasonable accommodations to augmented citizens, including:
- Assistive technology for communication
- Accessible evidence presentation
- Modifications to procedures where necessary
- Protection from discrimination in judicial processes

**Section 25.3** The right to accessible justice shall be recognized as a fundamental right.

**Constitutional Basis:** Sixth Amendment (trial); Seventh Amendment (jury); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process, Equal Protection)

---

## Article XXVI: Right to Development and Enhancement

**Section 26.1** Augmented citizens have the right to development and enhancement, including:
- Access to new augmentation technologies
- Right to upgrade and improve augmentations
- Right to pursue personal development through augmentation
- Right to mental and physical enhancement within safety standards

**Section 26.2** No entity may unreasonably restrict access to development and enhancement technologies.

**Section 26.3** The right to development and enhancement shall be balanced with safety and ethical considerations.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (liberty)

---

## Article XXVII: Right to Fair Treatment in Smart Cities

**Section 27.1** Augmented citizens have the right to fair treatment in smart cities, including:
- Access to smart-city services
- Equitable treatment in smart-city systems
- Privacy protections in smart-city data collection
- Non-discrimination in smart-city algorithms
- Participatory governance in smart-city decisions

**Section 27.2** Smart-city systems shall be designed to:
- Respect augmentation status neutrality
- Protect privacy and data sovereignty
- Provide reasonable accommodations
- Prevent discrimination and exclusion

**Section 27.3** Augmented citizens shall be included in the design and governance of smart-city systems.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Protection); Commerce Clause; Preamble (general welfare)

---

## Article XXVIII: Right to Privacy in Public Spaces

**Section 28.1** Augmented citizens have the right to privacy in public spaces, including:
- Protection from unauthorized neural surveillance
- Privacy of assistive technology use
- Control over personal data collection
- Protection from augmentation-based profiling

**Section 28.2** No entity may:
- Use surveillance technology to access neural data without consent
- Collect or process data from augmentations without consent
- Profile individuals based on augmentation status
- Use public space data to discriminate

**Section 28.3** The right to privacy in public spaces shall be subject to reasonable limitations for public safety.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fourth Amendment (privacy); Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article XXIX: Right to Informed Consent

**Section 29.1** Augmented citizens have the right to informed consent for any procedure, data collection, or action affecting their augmentations.

**Section 29.2** Informed consent shall require:
- Clear and accessible information
- Understanding of risks and benefits
- Voluntary agreement without coercion
- Right to withdraw consent at any time
- Documentation of consent

**Section 29.3** No entity may proceed without informed consent.

**Section 29.4** Informed consent shall be in a format accessible to the individual's assistive technology.

**Constitutional Basis:** Fifth Amendment (property); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process); Ninth Amendment (bodily autonomy)

## Article XXX: Right to Assistive Technology Maintenance and Support

**Section 30.1** Augmented citizens have the right to maintenance and support for their assistive technologies.

**Section 30.2** Maintenance and support shall be:
- Timely and responsive
- Accessible to the individual
- Privacy-preserving
- Cost-effective where subsidized
- Continuous and uninterrupted

**Section 30.3** No entity may:
- Deny maintenance or support without cause
- Require unreasonable procedures
- Charge discriminatory prices
- Withhold necessary repairs

**Section 30.4** The right to maintenance and support shall be recognized as necessary for the functioning of augmentations.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (Due Process)

## Article XXXVI: Right to Mental Integrity

**Section 36.1** Augmented citizens have the right to mental integrity, including:
- The right to maintain cognitive function
- The right to mental health and well-being
- The right to protection from cognitive interference
- The right to cognitive support where needed

**Section 36.2** No entity may:
- Interfere with cognitive function without consent
- Subject individuals to cognitive manipulation
- Use augmentations to control behavior
- Neglect cognitive health needs

**Section 36.3** The right to mental integrity shall be recognized as a fundamental right.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (liberty)

---

## Article XXXVII: Right to Future Generations

**Section 37.1** Augmented citizens have the right to consider future generations in decisions affecting augmentations, including:
- The right to ensure augmentation technologies are sustainable
- The right to preserve augmentation knowledge for future generations
- The right to participate in decisions affecting future augmentation availability
- The right to balance present needs with future possibilities

**Section 37.2** No entity may make decisions affecting augmentations that unreasonably prejudice future generations.

**Section 37.3** The right to future generations shall be balanced with present needs and rights.

**Constitutional Basis:** Preamble (general welfare); Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights)

---

## Article XXXVIII: Right to Dignity and Respect

**Section 38.1** Augmented citizens have the right to dignity and respect, including:
- The right to be treated with dignity
- The right to respect for augmentations
- The right to freedom from stigma
- The right to equal social standing

**Section 38.2** No entity may:
- Stigmatize augmented citizens
- Treat augmentations with disrespect
- Deny dignity based on augmentation status
- Dehumanize or objectify augmented individuals

**Section 38.3** The right to dignity and respect shall be recognized as a fundamental right.

**Constitutional Basis:** Preamble (blessings of liberty); Fourteenth Amendment (Equal Protection)

---

## Article XXXIX: Right to Exist and Be Recognized

**Section 39.1** Augmented citizens have the right to exist and be recognized as persons, including:
- The right to legal recognition
- The right to personhood regardless of augmentations
- The right to identity preservation
- The right to be treated as a person

**Section 39.2** No entity may:
- Deny personhood based on augmentations
- Treat augmentations as a limitation of personhood
- Dehumanize augmented individuals
- Deny recognition of identity

**Section 39.3** The right to exist and be recognized shall be recognized as the foundational right.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (citizenship, equal protection)

---

## Article XL: Right to Self-Determination

**Section 40.1** Augmented citizens have the right to self-determination, including:
- The right to define their own identity
- The right to choose their augmentations
- The right to determine their own future
- The right to participate in decisions affecting them

**Section 40.2** No entity may:
- Impose identity on augmented individuals
- Make decisions about augmentations without consent
- Determine the future of augmented individuals without their participation
- Deny self-determination rights

**Section 40.3** The right to self-determination shall be recognized as a fundamental right.

**Constitutional Basis:** Ninth Amendment (unenumerated rights); Fourteenth Amendment (liberty)

## Enforcement and Implementation

### Section 1: Office of Cybernetic and Augmented Rights (OCAR)

**1.1** There is hereby established the Office of Cybernetic and Augmented Rights (OCAR) within the Department of Justice.

**1.2** The Office shall:
- Investigate complaints of violations of rights under this Bill
- Enforce the provisions of this Bill through administrative action
- Advise government entities on compliance
- Educate the public about augmented rights
- Issue guidance and regulations
- Coordinate with other civil rights agencies

**1.3** The Office shall be headed by a Director appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate.

### Section 2: Private Right of Action

**2.1** Any person whose rights under this Bill are violated shall have a right of action in any court of competent jurisdiction.

**2.2** Remedies shall include:
- Injunctive relief
- Compensatory damages
- Punitive damages in cases of willful violation
- Attorney's fees and costs
- Such other relief as the court deems appropriate

**2.3** No person shall be required to exhaust administrative remedies before bringing an action.

### Section 3: Sovereign Immunity

**3.1** The rights under this Bill shall be enforceable against government entities.

**3.2** The protection of sovereign immunity is waived to the extent necessary to enforce this Bill.

**3.3** Government entities shall be liable for violations to the same extent as private entities.

### Section 4: Preemption

**4.1** This Bill shall preempt any state or local law that conflicts with its provisions.

**4.2** States may enact stronger protections, but may not diminish the protections provided by this Bill.

**4.3** The Supremacy Clause of Article VI shall govern any conflict.

### Section 5: Severability

**5.1** If any provision of this Bill is held invalid, the remainder shall continue in full force and effect.

**5.2** If any application of this Bill is held invalid, other applications shall continue in full force and effect.

### Section 6: Effective Date

**6.1** This Bill shall take effect 180 days after its enactment.

**6.2** Government entities shall have 180 days to comply with the accessibility requirements.

**6.3** Private entities shall have 180 days to comply with the non-discrimination requirements.

### Section 7: Rulemaking Authority

**7.1** The Director of OCAR shall have authority to issue regulations implementing this Bill.

**7.2** Regulations shall be issued after notice and comment.

**7.3** Regulations shall be consistent with the purposes and provisions of this Bill.

## Appendix B: Integration with Existing Legal Frameworks

### Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)

**Title II:** Public entities shall provide auxiliary aids and services where necessary to ensure effective communication and equal access.

**Title III:** Public accommodations shall provide reasonable modifications to policies, practices, and procedures.

**ADA Amendments Act of 2008:** The ameliorative effects of mitigating measures shall not be considered when determining disability.

**Integration:** Augmentations and assistive technologies shall be treated as mitigating measures that do not reduce ADA coverage.

### Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act

**Purpose:** To prohibit discrimination on the basis of disability in programs receiving federal funding.

**Integration:** Augmented citizens shall be protected under Section 504, and augmentations shall be treated as disabilities requiring reasonable accommodation.

### Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act

**Purpose:** To require federal agencies to make electronic and information technology accessible.

**Integration:** Augmented citizens shall have the right to accessible technology from federal agencies.

### Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)

**Article 19:** Right to live independently and be included in the community.

**Article 22:** Respect for privacy, including personal, health, and rehabilitation information.

**Integration:** Augmented citizens shall have the rights recognized under the CRPD, including community inclusion and privacy.

### General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

**Principles:** Data minimization, purpose limitation, explicit consent, right to deletion.

**Integration:** Augmented citizens shall have the same data protection rights as all individuals.

### National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

**Purpose:** To develop cybersecurity and privacy standards.

**Integration:** Augmentations shall comply with NIST standards for security and privacy.
